Stairio V7
Ventilation sensitivity

What-if calculations, not measured operating temperatures. The real PLA casing mesh is coupled to a perfectly mixed internal-air heat balance. Airflow and heat loads are assumptions. This model does not calculate fan pressure, local velocity, recirculation patterns, individual component temperatures or component-to-shell contact hot spots.

What changed from the first shell study

The first study put 1 W directly into selected PLA walls. This study releases hypothetical heat into internal air, which can lose heat through both ventilation and the shell. The two cases have different heat paths and should not be compared as if a physical cooling modification was tested.

ΔT_air = P / (G_shell + ρ c_p V_fresh)
T_shell(node) = T_room + θ(node) × ΔT_air

The reference shell conductance is 0.065415 W/K; ventilation adds 0.562138 W/K for each assumed CFM. The normalized shell field θ comes from internal bulk air at 1°C and outside air at 0°C. These computational reference temperatures give a 1 K difference; constant-property conduction/convection allows an exact shift to the scenario temperatures.

Assumptions and scope

  • PLA conductivity 0.2 W/(m·K), homogeneous and isotropic; printed material not measured.
  • Selected inner wall area 0.0213920 m² and outer wall area 0.0246259 m². Other shell faces insulated; other ten housing bodies suppressed, including lids and internal parts. No radiation.
  • Uniform internal film coefficients 5/10/20 and exterior coefficients 5/10 W/(m²·K), varied independently of airflow. For 10 W and 1 CFM, these cases give bulk air 39.6–41.3°C and shell peaks 33.6–39.4°C. These ranges cover the chosen assumptions only.
  • Constant air density 1.184 kg/m³ and specific heat 1006 J/(kg·K), approximate room-temperature dry air values.
  • All heat enters perfectly mixed air; no direct component contacts. Perfect mixing can underpredict local hot spots, so the result is not a guaranteed upper temperature bound.
  • Zero net exchange is included separately in the raw results as an extrapolation diagnostic. It is not necessarily a stopped fan. Very high calculated temperatures signal the need for more complete physics, not a physically stable PLA shape.
  • No pass/fail claim for PLA or electronics is made. A 50°C flag in the data is an analyst review marker, not a filament specification or allowable operating temperature.

Geometry reconciliation

The user-confirmed fan-4007 maps to housing Solid5 and assembly Solid17. Its housing-local origin is [0, −28.5, 112.5] mm, with rotor axis parallel to Z. Flow direction and pressure-flow curve remain unknown.

The standalone housing includes the primary shell, but the 235-body full-assembly import has no matching shell. All ten other housing bodies match after transforming coordinates. Historical records describe the shell as visible and unsuppressed, so the import/configuration discrepancy remains unresolved. Three internal boards are identical 60 × 74 × 5.6 mm placeholders and do not establish Pi/C610/power-manager geometry.

Some apparent side vents are shallow grooves. A future CFD volume must reconcile the shell, cap, occupied camera openings and internal obstructions before treating openings as connected air passages. Fluent 2025 R2 terminal startup was successfully verified; no CFD flow-field solve is claimed here.

Numerical verification

All seven ANSYS basis solves completed. Independent integration of both convection surfaces gives a worst relative heat imbalance of 0.0000232%. Refining from 84,293 to 135,330 nodes changes shell conductance by 0.0293% and normalized peak temperature by 0.0017%. These are local two-mesh sensitivity checks.

The representative physical-temperature solve gives a 38.11145°C peak, differing from the linear calculation by 0.0000007°C. Its solver log reports zero errors and preserves two warnings concerning Workbench shape checking and surface-element material energy output. Volumetric PLA conductivity is explicitly assigned.

Final independent scenario/direct-solve verification: PASS. See the linked audit for heat integrals and tolerances.

Next evidence to improve this model

  1. Measure net air exchange, or identify the exact fan variant, supply voltage, flow direction and pressure-flow curve.
  2. Measure heat dissipated inside during representative operation, separating motor shaft output and regulator throughput from losses.
  3. Reconcile current internal hardware and connected vent openings; then build the complete CFD air volume.
  4. Compare predicted bulk and shell temperatures with measurements before judging casing changes.
